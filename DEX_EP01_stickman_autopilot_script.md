### THE PROBLEM · 0:45 to 1:30
**SHOT:** **B1** (POV typing) → mock-up of a scene-by-scene prompt list with red ✕ marks → **E-Thoughtful** expression cutaway.
**TEXT:** `Voice changes` · `Narration cut off` · `Found out after rendering`

> **DEX:** If you've made AI animations scene by scene, you know the pain. You write a story. You prompt scene one, then scene two. The voice changes halfway through. The narration runs longer than the clip, so the last word gets cut off. And you only find out after you've paid for the render. Multiply that by every scene, and a two-minute cartoon eats your whole evening and a good chunk of your credits. The fix isn't a better prompt. It's splitting the job in two: Claude does the thinking, and a Flow tool does the making.

### HOW IT WORKS · 1:30 to 2:10
**SHOT:** **P-Present** pose (open-hand gesture) beside an animated three-box diagram: `CLAUDE` → `CHECK` → `FLOW TOOL`. Boxes glow neon green, electric blue and warm amber in turn.

> **DEX:** Here's the whole system on one screen. Part one is Claude. You give it a single command and it writes the story, breaks it into scenes, writes the narration for each one, and picks one narrator voice for the whole film. Part two is a custom tool inside Google Flow. Flow Tools let you build your own workflow just by describing it, and this one takes Claude's scenes and renders them in order. Part three is the safety check in between. We'll get to that, because that's the bit that saves your credits.

### STEP 1: BUILD THE FLOW TOOL · 2:10 to 3:20
**SHOT:** **SCREEN** (your recording): Google Flow → new tool → typing the description. Picture-in-picture of **A1** in the bottom-right corner.
**TEXT:** Each setting pops up as a neon chip while Dex reads it out.

> **DEX:** Step one. Open Google Flow and start a new tool. You don't code this. You describe it. Here's roughly what I told it. Take a list of scenes, each with an action and a line of narration. Generate the narration first, with one chosen voice. Set each clip length to the narration length plus a short gap. Start the voice a fraction of a second after the picture. Keep the background ambience low under the voice. And before anything renders, check every scene for problems and lock the Start button until they're fixed.
>

## 5. Starter Claude command (shown on screen in Step 2, edit to fit your tool)

```
Write a 4-scene stickman story about [IDEA].
Use one narrator voice for the whole film.
For each scene give me:
- ACTION: what happens on screen (one or two sentences)
- NARRATION: one line, under 120 characters
Then open my [TOOL NAME] tool in Google Flow, fill in every scene,
check there are no warnings, and press Start.
```

The last two lines depend on Claude being able to operate Google Flow in your setup (for example through a browser-control tool). If it can't, Claude outputs the scenes and you paste them into the tool yourself. Say so on camera if that's how you record it.
